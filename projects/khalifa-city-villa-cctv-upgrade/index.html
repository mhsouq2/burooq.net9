Khalifa City Villa CCTV Upgrade: Closing 4 Blind Spots With an 8-Camera 4K System

A 5-bedroom villa with a side alley, a rear garden gate and a long driveway had exactly one working camera left, and it didn't cover any of them. Here's the full site assessment, the system we designed, and what changed after installation.

Location

Khalifa City, Abu Dhabi

Property

5-bedroom villa, corner plot

Timeline

2 working days

The situation

The homeowner had an older 4-camera analog system installed by a previous contractor when the villa was first built. Two cameras had failed outright, one covered a wall that had since been extended, and the fourth pointed at the garage rather than the gate. In practice, the villa had almost no working coverage, and the family only discovered this after checking footage following a delivery dispute and finding nothing usable.

Because it's a corner plot, the property has a longer-than-average perimeter, a side pedestrian gate that isn't visible from the house, and a rear garden gate that opens onto a service road, three access points a standard 4-camera villa package would not adequately cover.

Site assessment

Before quoting, our team walked the full perimeter and mapped it against camera field-of-view rather than just counting existing camera positions. This surfaced four specific blind spots:

  • The side pedestrian gate, unlit at night and not visible from any existing camera angle.
  • The rear garden gate opening onto the service road, a common secondary entry point installers often miss.
  • A shaded section of the driveway where the old camera's night vision range fell short.
  • The main entrance door, previously covered from too wide an angle to read faces or parcels clearly.

We used the same logic behind our camera coverage calculator to check overlap between proposed camera positions before finalizing the layout, so no two cameras duplicated the same view while leaving these four points genuinely covered.

The system we designed

8x 4K PoE turret & bullet cameras

4 perimeter bullet cameras with 30m+ night vision range, 4 turret cameras at entrances and gates.

16-channel NVR, 4TB storage

Sized using our storage calculator for roughly 25 days of continuous recording across all 8 cameras.

PoE switch & structured cabling

New cable runs to the side and rear gates rather than relying on the previous, undersized wiring.

Mobile app with push alerts

Live viewing and motion alerts configured on the homeowner's and spouse's phones, with gate-specific alert zones.

Coverage went from effectively zero working blind-spot cameras to full perimeter coverage: main gate, side pedestrian gate, rear garden gate, driveway, front door and two garden-facing angles.

Installation timeline

  1. Day 1 morning: cabling

    Ran new PoE cable to the side and rear gate positions, since the existing cabling didn't reach either point.

  2. Day 1 afternoon: mounting & NVR setup

    Mounted all 8 cameras at surveyed heights and angles, configured the NVR and storage schedule.

  3. Day 2 morning: testing & adjustment

    Checked night vision range and field of view at dusk for every camera, adjusted two angles that had minor glare from a garden light.

  4. Day 2 afternoon: handover

    Walked the homeowner through the mobile app, alert zones and playback, and left the old analog system's cabling in place as a spare conduit for future use.

Result

All four previous blind spots are now covered, including the two gates that had no camera coverage at all before this project. The homeowner can view any entrance live from their phone, and motion alerts are scoped to the gates specifically, so they aren't getting notifications every time a car passes on the street outside. Footage from the driveway camera, previously unusable after dark, is now clear enough to read a number plate at night.

"We didn't realize how little of the property the old system actually covered until the team walked us through it camera by camera. Now I can check both gates from my phone before I even get to the intercom."

— Homeowner, Khalifa City villa (name withheld for privacy)

What this project shows for similar villas

  • Camera count isn't the same as coverage. This villa technically had 4 cameras before, and still had zero usable coverage of either gate.
  • Corner and multi-gate plots need a proper walkthrough, not a standard package sized for a rectangular single-gate villa.
  • Night vision range matters as much as camera count, particularly along driveways and unlit side gates.
  • Cabling is often the real constraint, not the cameras themselves; older villas frequently need new runs to reach gates added or modified after the original build.
