Quick answer: most off-the-shelf "smart thermostats" are built for wall-mounted split units with an infrared remote, and won't work with a ducted system out of the box. Why Ducted AC Is Different From a Wall-Mounted Split

A wall-mounted split unit has a visible indoor unit with an infrared receiver, which is exactly what most consumer smart thermostats and universal IR controllers are designed to talk to. A ducted (concealed) system is different: the indoor unit sits hidden in the false ceiling, air is distributed through ductwork to multiple rooms, and the only user-facing control is a wired wall controller — there's no exposed unit for an IR device to "see." That's why a generic smart thermostat bought off a shelf usually won't connect to a ducted system, even though it works fine on a normal split unit in the next room.

What's Actually Possible: Mitsubishi Electric

Mitsubishi Electric ducted systems can typically be fitted with a wireless interface or gateway accessory that connects directly to the indoor unit's control board, alongside your existing wired wall controller. Once installed and configured, this allows scheduling, remote on/off, and temperature control from a mobile app rather than only from the physical controller on the wall. Exact compatibility depends on your specific indoor unit model and its control board revision, which we confirm on-site before quoting — this is not a plug-and-play retrofit like it would be on a basic split unit.

What's Actually Possible: O General

O General ducted units are commonly run from a wired remote controller mounted on the wall. Adding app-based smart control means wiring a compatible interface module into the indoor unit's control terminals, rather than relying on an infrared blaster the unit can't see. Once wired in, the module bridges the AC system to WiFi so it can be scheduled and monitored remotely, working alongside the existing wired controller rather than replacing it.

What This Retrofit Actually Involves

  1. Site visit and model check

    Confirming the exact indoor unit model, control board version, and existing wiring at the wall controller.

  2. Compatibility confirmation

    Matching the correct interface module for your specific Mitsubishi Electric or O General model before ordering parts.

  3. Module installation

    Wiring the smart interface module into the indoor unit's control board, typically accessed via the false ceiling or the existing wall controller cabling.

  4. Network connection

    Connecting the module to your home WiFi and pairing it with the manufacturer's app (or a compatible third-party app where supported).

  5. Scheduling setup

    Setting up zone-by-zone schedules and remote alerts based on how the property is actually used.

  6. Testing and handover

    Confirming the wired wall controller and the app both control the unit correctly, with a walkthrough of the app.

Does This Replace Your Existing Wall Controller?

No — the smart interface module works alongside your existing wired wall controller, not instead of it. You keep manual control on the wall exactly as before, and gain scheduling, remote access, and monitoring from your phone on top of it. This also means if you're already running a broader smart home automation system, your ducted AC zones can usually be brought into the same app rather than living in a separate one.
