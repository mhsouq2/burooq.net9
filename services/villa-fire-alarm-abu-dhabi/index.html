Why villa owners in Abu Dhabi are adding fire alarms now

Since 1 January 2024 every villa in the UAE must have a fire alarm connected to Civil Defence monitoring (the Hassantuk programme). New villas, extensions and villas with annexes or a separate majlis also have their fire alarm drawings and installation checked before handover, and buildings must keep fire systems maintained under a valid maintenance contract.

Whether you need a few detectors for a finished villa or a full system for a new build, we survey the villa, recommend the right type of system and give you a clear quote.

Every type of fire alarm device for villas

Smoke detectors

Optical smoke detectors for bedrooms, living rooms, majlis and corridors: the first warning of a fire.

Heat detectors

For kitchens, garages and laundry rooms, where steam or cooking smoke would cause false alarms.

Multi-sensor detectors

Smoke and heat in one device, with fewer false alarms in tricky areas.

Gas leak detectors

For kitchens with LPG cylinders, with an optional automatic shut-off valve.

Call points and sounders

Break-glass call points, sounders and flashing strobes so everyone notices the alarm.

Wireless detectors

No cables and no damage to finished ceilings, ideal for existing villas.

Conventional or addressable fire alarm panel?

Conventional fire alarm panel

Detectors are wired in zones, for example ground floor, first floor and annex. When a detector goes off, the panel shows which zone it is in. Conventional panels are simpler and lower in cost, and suit most small and medium villas.

Addressable fire alarm panel

Every detector has its own address, so the panel shows the exact room, such as "Bedroom 3, first floor". Addressable panels suit large villas, palaces, compounds and buildings, and make faults and maintenance faster to trace.

Wireless and hybrid systems

Wireless detectors talk to the panel by radio, so there is nothing to chase into walls. They are the cleanest option for finished villas.

Civil Defence inspection checklist for villas

  • A smoke detector in every bedroom, living area, majlis and corridor, and a heat detector in the kitchen
  • Devices listed and approved by Civil Defence, not online or unbranded alarms
  • Panel showing normal, with no fault or trouble lights, and working backup batteries
  • Every detector tested, clean and not painted over or covered
  • Approved drawings for new builds and extensions, matching what is installed
  • A valid maintenance contract with a Civil Defence-approved company, with test records

Always ask any fire alarm company for its Abu Dhabi Civil Defence approval before you sign. In Abu Dhabi, buildings without a valid approved maintenance contract for fire alarm systems face a fine of AED 10,000.
