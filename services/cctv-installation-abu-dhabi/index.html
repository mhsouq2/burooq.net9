Which camera type does your property need?

Most jobs use a mix of camera types rather than one type throughout. Here's how we typically split them:

Camera type Best for Why
DomeIndoor areas, majlis, entrancesDiscreet, vandal-resistant housing, wide-angle coverage
BulletPerimeters, driveways, gatesLonger visible range, clearly visible as a deterrent
PTZ (pan-tilt-zoom)Warehouses, compounds, large open yardsCan be steered manually or set to auto-track movement across a wide area
TurretBalconies, low walls, tight cornersAdjustable angle without the dome's IR-reflection issue on glass

We install IP (network) cameras for most jobs since they hold detail better at range and integrate directly with phone apps and NVR storage. Analog HD-TVI/HD-CVI systems remain an option on tighter budgets or where coaxial cabling already exists — we'll tell you honestly which fits your property rather than upselling by default. Our installation process

  1. Free site survey. An engineer walks the property, checks lighting and cable routes, and identifies blind spots you'd otherwise only discover after something goes missing.
  2. System design & quote. Camera count, type, NVR channels and storage size are specified in writing before any work starts — no vague "package" pricing.
  3. Installation. Cameras mounted, cabling run and concealed where possible, NVR configured on-site.
  4. Testing. Every camera checked for coverage, focus and night vision before we consider the job done.
  5. Remote access setup. Phone app configured and tested on your device, not just left for you to figure out.
  6. Handover & AMC option. Walkthrough of playback and alerts, plus the option to start an Annual Maintenance Contract for ongoing coverage.

What affects CCTV installation cost in Abu Dhabi

We don't publish a flat price because it isn't an honest number — a 4-camera apartment job and an 8-camera villa with long cable runs cost differently for real reasons. What actually moves the price:

  • Camera count and resolution — more cameras and higher resolution (4K vs standard HD) both raise cost.
  • Cable run length and difficulty — concealed conduit through finished walls costs more labor than surface trunking.
  • NVR channels and storage size — driven by camera count and how long you want to retain footage.
  • PTZ or specialty cameras — cost more per unit than fixed dome or bullet cameras.
  • Remote access and cloud backup — configuration is included, but cloud storage subscriptions (if chosen) are billed separately by the provider.

For the full breakdown with worked examples, read our CCTV installation cost guide, or skip straight to a firm number with a free site survey.

Common CCTV installation mistakes we get called to fix

  • Blind spots at entry points — cameras aimed at the driveway but not the actual front door or side gate.
  • Undersized NVR storage — footage gets overwritten in days instead of weeks because retention wasn't calculated against camera count and resolution.
  • Exposed, unprotected cabling — left loose along walls or ceilings, an easy target for tampering and weather damage.
  • Remote access never configured — the system records locally but no one ever set up the phone app, so nobody actually checks it.
  • Cameras marketed as "HD" that are older analog units — technically accurate, practically low-resolution when you need to identify a face or plate later.
