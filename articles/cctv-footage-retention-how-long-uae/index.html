How Long Is CCTV Footage Kept in the UAE — and Who Can Legally Request It?

"The incident happened three weeks ago — is the footage still there?" is one of the most common calls we get, and it usually comes right after something's already gone wrong, which is the worst time to discover the answer is no. Retention isn't governed by a fixed legal number in the UAE; it's governed entirely by how the system was sized and configured. Here's what actually determines it, typical windows by setup type, and who can legitimately ask for footage once it exists.

This isn't legal advice — for a genuine dispute or formal request, a lawyer is the right call — but as installers who size systems and field these calls every week, here's the practical picture.

What actually determines how long footage lasts

Almost every CCTV system records in a continuous loop: once the storage is full, the oldest footage is automatically overwritten to make room for new recordings, with no warning before it happens. How long that loop lasts before looping back depends on four things working together:

  • Hard drive size — the single biggest factor. A larger drive simply buys more days before overwriting starts.
  • Camera count and resolution — more cameras, or higher-resolution 4K cameras, generate more data per hour and fill storage faster than fewer, lower-resolution ones.
  • Recording mode — continuous 24/7 recording fills storage far faster than motion-triggered recording, which only writes footage when something actually happens in frame.
  • Compression (H.265 vs H.264) — newer H.265 encoding can roughly halve file size compared to older H.264 at the same quality, effectively doubling how many days a given drive can hold.

Our CCTV storage calculator lets you plug in your actual camera count, resolution, and recording mode to see the real number of days a given hard drive will hold — it's usually a very different figure from what people assume.

Typical retention windows by setup

  • Small shop or apartment, 4 cameras, motion-triggered, 2TB drive: commonly 30-45 days.
  • Villa, 8-16 cameras, mixed continuous/motion, 4-6TB drive: commonly 20-40 days, depending heavily on how many cameras run continuous 24/7 versus motion-only.
  • Warehouse or labor camp, many cameras, continuous recording, 8TB+ with RAID: commonly 30-60+ days, since these sites often deliberately size storage larger given the higher stakes of an incident.
  • Cloud-subscription cameras: retention is set entirely by the subscription tier, typically 7, 14, or 30 days, and once that window passes the footage is gone from the cloud regardless of local storage.

A reasonable practical target for most UAE homes and businesses is around 30 days of retention — enough time for most incidents to be noticed and footage requested, without needing an oversized, costlier drive.

Who can legitimately request your CCTV footage

As the property owner, you control your own footage, and you're not generally obligated to hand it over to just anyone who asks. That said, several requests come up regularly and are worth knowing how to handle:

  • Police investigating an incident — a break-in, accident, or dispute reported through proper channels. This is the most common and most legitimate reason footage gets requested, and cooperating through the formal reporting process is the right approach.
  • Your insurer following a claim — footage supporting a burglary, accident, or damage claim is often requested as part of the claims process, and having it available and easy to export can materially speed up a payout.
  • A landlord or community/HOA management, in specific documented disputes — usually limited to footage genuinely relevant to the issue at hand, not a general request.
  • A neighbor or third party asking informally — you're not obligated to share footage just because someone asks, especially if it shows other identifiable people; directing them to file a police report if it's a genuine incident is usually the appropriate response.

If you're ever unsure whether a request is legitimate, it's reasonable to ask for it in writing or to direct the requester through the police, rather than handing over footage on the spot.

How to avoid losing footage you actually need

  1. Export or back up a clip as soon as an incident happens — don't wait, since the loop will keep overwriting in the background regardless of whether you've reviewed it yet.
  2. Size storage with a realistic buffer, not the bare minimum, so a delayed report (a scratch on a car noticed days later, a dispute raised weeks after the fact) doesn't fall outside the recorded window.
  3. Use motion-triggered recording on lower-priority cameras to stretch retention further without sacrificing coverage on the areas that matter most.
  4. Keep a note of your system's actual retention window so you know immediately whether a given request is still within reach, rather than discovering the footage is already gone.

Getting the sizing right from the start

Retention is one of the most overlooked decisions in a CCTV installation — most people focus on camera count and quality and only think about storage duration after they've needed footage that wasn't there. During a site survey, we size the hard drive to a specific retention target based on your camera count, resolution, and recording mode, so the number of days you actually get matches what you expect.
