Does CCTV Record Audio? What UAE Homeowners and Businesses Should Know

Some CCTV cameras can record sound, many can't, and recording conversations carries real privacy risk in the UAE. Here's how to check your cameras and when audio should be switched off.

Written by Alburooq's CCTV installation team in Abu Dhabi

Short answer

Many modern CCTV cameras can record audio — especially Wi-Fi cameras, video doorbells and newer IP cameras with built-in microphones. Traditional analog cameras and many professional bullet and dome cameras do not record sound unless a separate microphone is connected. Whether audio is actually recorded depends on the camera model and the recorder or app settings.

In the UAE, privacy law is strict and recording people's conversations without their knowledge can expose you to legal risk. For most homes and businesses, the safe choice is video only, with audio switched off, unless you have a specific, legitimate need and have taken advice.

How to tell if your CCTV records sound

  • Check the camera: a small pinhole marked "MIC" or a two-way talk feature means it has a microphone.
  • Check the app or NVR: in Hikvision, Dahua, UNV, Imou, Ezviz and similar apps, look for "Audio", "Audio Encoding" or a speaker icon on playback.
  • Play back a recording: if you hear sound on playback, audio is being recorded and stored.
  • Doorbells and Wi-Fi cameras usually have audio enabled by default — check these first.

Why audio recording is a privacy risk in the UAE

The UAE treats privacy seriously. Recording or sharing other people's private conversations without consent, or publishing footage of identifiable people, can create legal problems under UAE privacy and cybercrime laws, even on your own property. Video recording of your own entrances and property for security is common and accepted, but microphones pick up conversations of domestic workers, visitors, neighbours and passers-by, which is where the risk rises.

Businesses should be particularly careful: staff and customers have privacy expectations, and some sectors have their own CCTV rules. Read our overview of UAE CCTV privacy laws. This article is general information, not legal advice — for a specific situation, speak to a qualified UAE lawyer.

Our recommendation

  1. Disable audio recording on cameras by default.
  2. Point cameras at your own property — entrances, gates, parking, cash counters — not neighbours or public spaces.
  3. Never place cameras in bedrooms, bathrooms or changing areas.
  4. Display a "CCTV in operation" sign at business premises.
  5. Keep footage secure and only share it with the police or authorities when needed.
