How Much Does a Website Cost in Abu Dhabi? A Plain-Language Price Guide for Small Businesses

Ask five companies for a website quote and you may get five answers, from a few hundred dirhams to tens of thousands. This guide explains what you actually pay for, what a fair price looks like for a small business, and which yearly costs people forget.

Written by Alburooq's web team in Abu Dhabi

Short answer

For a small business in Abu Dhabi, typical website prices are:

  • One-page business website: about AED 1,500 – 3,000
  • Business website, 5–10 pages, SEO-ready: about AED 3,500 – 8,000
  • Online store with payments or WhatsApp orders: about AED 6,000 – 15,000

On top of the build, expect a few small yearly costs: the domain name, hosting and security updates. Website care usually starts from about AED 150 a month. Bigger custom platforms with logins, bookings or links to other systems cost more, often much more.

The price depends far more on what is included than on who builds it. That is why comparing quotes line by line matters.

What you are really paying for

A website quote is a bundle of separate jobs. Cheap quotes leave some of them out; expensive ones sometimes include things you do not need. Here is what each part means.

Design and layout

Either a ready template adjusted with your logo and colours, or a design made for your business. A good template is fine for most small companies. What matters is that it looks clean on a phone, because most of your visitors will arrive from Google or WhatsApp on mobile.

Content: text and photos

This is the part owners underestimate. Someone has to write clear service descriptions, prices or "from" prices, opening hours and answers to common questions. If the company writes it for you, in proper Arabic and English, the price goes up, but so do the results. Real photos of your shop, team and work beat stock photos every time.

Arabic and English

A real bilingual site is not English with a translate button. The Arabic version needs a right-to-left layout, correct alignment, and text written or checked by an Arabic speaker. Expect a bilingual site to cost more than a single-language one, because it is close to building the content twice.

SEO foundations

"SEO-ready" should mean concrete things: a proper title and description on each page, fast loading, correct headings, a sitemap, the site added to Google Search Console, and structured data so Google understands your business. Without these, a beautiful site can sit on page five of Google.

Features

Each feature adds work: a booking form, an online store with payment gateway, a customer login, a catalogue of hundreds of products, or a link to your POS or accounting system. Keep the first version simple and add features once the site is bringing enquiries.

Which one fits your business?

  • One page suits a garage in Mussafah, a salon, a tailoring shop or a home-maintenance business. Visitors want to know what you do, where you are and how to call you. One well-made page answers all three.
  • 5–10 pages suits a company with several distinct services, where each service deserves its own page so it can rank for its own searches (for example "CCTV installation" and "intercom repair" on separate pages).
  • Online store suits shops that want to sell or take orders online. Many UAE stores start with "order on WhatsApp" buttons and add card payments later.

The costs people forget

Domain name

Your address on the internet, such as yourcompany.ae or yourcompany.com. According to the UAE government portal, the .ae namespace is run by the .ae Domain Administration (aeDA) under TDRA, and you register a .ae name through one of its accredited registrars. There is also an Arabic domain ending, .امارات. Whatever ending you choose, the most important rule is simple: the domain must be registered in your company's name, not the designer's. If the designer disappears, you should still own your address.

Hosting and security updates

The site has to live on a server, and its software needs regular updates and backups. A site that is never updated is the one that gets hacked or quietly breaks. Ask who handles this after launch and what it costs per month or year.

Changes after launch

New prices, new photos, a Ramadan offer, a new branch. Ask whether small edits are included in the care plan or charged one by one.

Business email

An address like info@yourcompany.ae looks more trustworthy than a free email. It is usually a separate small monthly fee per mailbox from the email provider.

UAE-specific things your website should get right

WhatsApp first

In the UAE, many customers would rather send a WhatsApp message than fill in a form. Put a WhatsApp button and a tap-to-call button on every page, visible without scrolling on a phone. This one detail often decides whether a visit turns into a lead.

Arabic and English families

Many households in Abu Dhabi use both languages, and the person who searches is not always the person who decides. A clear language switch, with Arabic that reads naturally, widens your audience.

Mobile speed

People search from the car park, from a site visit, or from home on a busy network. Heavy videos and huge images slow the site down. Ask your designer to test on an ordinary phone on mobile data, not only on an office computer.

Your area names

Customers search with places: "laptop repair Khalifa City", "CCTV MBZ City". Mention the areas you really serve, such as Khalifa City, Mussafah or Al Ain, so Google can match you to local searches.

Customer data and privacy

If your site collects names, phone numbers or emails through a form, the UAE's personal data protection law, Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021, applies to that data. The government summary of the law says personal data should not be processed without the owner's consent, with limited exceptions, and that companies must keep it secure and confidential. In practice: add a short privacy policy, collect only what you need, and use a secure (https) site. For your specific obligations, ask a legal adviser.

How to compare website quotes: 7 questions to ask

  1. How many pages, and which ones? Get the list in writing.
  2. Who writes the text, and in which languages? "Bilingual" should mean both languages are complete.
  3. What does "SEO-ready" include? Titles, descriptions, sitemap, Search Console, speed, structured data.
  4. Is the domain registered in my name? The answer must be yes.
  5. What are the yearly costs after launch? Domain, hosting, updates, email.
  6. Can I see two or three live sites you built? Open them on your own phone.
  7. What happens if I want to move the site later? You should be able to take your content and domain with you.

A quote that answers all seven clearly is worth more than a cheaper one that answers three.

Website alone is not enough: add your Google Business Profile

For local businesses, many calls come from the map results when someone searches "near me". A complete Google Business Profile with the right category, services, opening hours, real photos and a link to your website works hand in hand with the site. Set it up at the same time as the website; it typically costs AED 300 – 750 if you want it done for you.
