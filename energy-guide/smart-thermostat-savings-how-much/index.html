Smart Thermostat Savings: How Much Can You Realistically Expect?

"Save up to 50%!" claims are everywhere in smart home marketing. Here's what the actual, cited data says instead.

What ENERGY STAR and the DOE Actually Report

ENERGY STAR reports that certified smart thermostats save an average of approximately 8% on heating and cooling costs, translating to roughly $50 per year for a typical household. The U.S. Department of Energy separately reports that setting your thermostat back 7-10°F for eight hours a day, exactly what smart scheduling automates, can save approximately 10% annually on heating and cooling.

Some real-world reports go higher: Duke Energy has cited smart thermostat customers seeing cooling cost reductions as high as 20% when combining scheduling with remote/geofenced control, since the biggest gains come from eliminating the "cooling an empty house all day" pattern entirely, not just nudging the setpoint a couple of degrees.

The honest range: 8-10% is the well-documented average. 15-20% is achievable specifically for households replacing genuinely inefficient prior scheduling (or none at all). Neither number is "up to 50%" — and any single device claiming that in isolation is overselling.

Getting the Full 8-10%, Not Just Part of It

The DOE's 10% figure assumes the setback is actually applied consistently, for the full 8 hours, every day. A schedule set once and never checked tends to drift, get overridden, or simply stop matching your actual routine after a few months — which is why our full guide dedicates an entire chapter to geofencing specifically, since it automates the "actually apply it every day" part that a static schedule alone doesn't guarantee.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a smart thermostat really save 50% on electricity?

No credible source supports a blanket 50% figure. ENERGY STAR reports ~8% average savings on heating/cooling costs; DOE reports up to 10% from consistent setback scheduling. Some households see more, particularly if replacing no schedule at all, but 50% is not a realistic average expectation.

How long until a smart thermostat pays for itself?

At $50/year in average savings against a $100-300 device cost, typical payback is 6 months to 2 years depending on the specific model and household.

Do I need a C-wire for a smart thermostat?

Many do, though several models include a power adapter kit or bypass workaround for homes without one. Confirm compatibility with your specific HVAC system before buying.
